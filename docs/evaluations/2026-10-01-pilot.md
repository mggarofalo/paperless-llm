# First Luna prompt tuning pilot

The selected experimental prompt substantially improved preservation of existing metadata, but **did not meet OCR acceptance**. It remains an [offline candidate](../../eval/prompts/preserve-context-v1.txt), not the worker default. No experimental output was applied to Paperless and no release was published from this experiment.

## Method

Twenty real documents were selected as a stratified pilot: 16 training and four holdout documents. The six known problematic worker outputs were assigned to training. This was a purposeful small sample, not a random estimate of accuracy across the archive. The affected documents were restored from Paperless history and verified before exporting the corpus. Original files and object permissions were preserved.

The fixed corpus included receipts, statements, invoices, forms, a check, a business card, and multipage material. Private scans, metadata, annotations, exports, inference logs, and results remain outside this repository. Astra and separate Luna agents labeled selected fields and literal OCR facts from the scans. These are model-assisted reference labels with visual spot checks, not a professionally transcribed gold corpus.

Luna ran through Codex CLI 0.159.2 with medium reasoning, a fresh ephemeral session for every case, and no observed tool calls. The production payload builder, schema, validator, and page images were reused. The deployment uses Pi 0.99.2; system-message placement, token limits, and image handling are not proven identical. These results do not establish production-runner parity.

The hillclimb evaluated baseline plus four revisions on training only:

1. Preserve salient visible identifiers in titles.
2. Add direct numeric and table OCR checks.
3. Preserve informative owner titles even when their context is absent from the scan; avoid duplicate tags; distinguish decorative image elements from text.
4. Rewrite as a shorter decision sequence with explicit valid keep/set shapes and a final validity check.

The fourth revision was repeated, then frozen before holdout evaluation at `2026-10-01T21:13:16Z`. Its instruction-file SHA-256 is `E9ED3C8192A8B0146582C1AFAE2ACA5843F47EBBE84F6DBD470A8CB28CFFD251`. Selection favored valid proposals, preservation of curated metadata, and improved OCR fact coverage. Search stopped after four revisions; no prompt was changed in response to holdout results.

The root selector did not inspect holdout labels until after freezing. One prompt-design subagent accidentally searched holdout files after delivering its original training candidates. That agent was stopped and excluded from further design; it provided no holdout content to the selector. This is imperfect team-level blinding and should be corrected with stronger data isolation in the next experiment.

## Results

All counts below include production-validator rejection as failure. Fact scores are matched reference substrings in valid proposals' resulting OCR; invalid proposals receive zero. They are not character accuracy or a guarantee of full transcription.

| Training version | Valid proposals | Titles preserved | OCR facts matched | Cases passing every required check |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Baseline | 16/16 | 4/16 | 59/81 | 3/16 |
| Revision 1 | 13/16 | 1/16 | 44/81 | 1/16 |
| Revision 2 | 16/16 | 1/16 | 60/81 | 1/16 |
| Revision 3 | 13/16 | 13/16 | 50/81 | 7/16 |
| Revision 4 | 16/16 | 16/16 | 61/81 | 7/16 |
| Revision 4 repeat | 16/16 | 16/16 | 66/81 | 9/16 |

| Reserved holdout | Valid proposals | Titles preserved | OCR facts matched | Cases passing every required check |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Baseline | 2/4 | 0/4 | 9/23 | 0/4 |
| Frozen revision 4 | 4/4 | 4/4 | 19/23 | 2/4 |

The selected prompt preserved the referenced dates, correspondents, and document types on all scored real cases. Those fields were already mostly correct, so this is primarily a preservation test. Four additional [synthetic text-only cases](../../eval/synthetic-cases.jsonl) tested wrong metadata, a placeholder title, a missing descriptive tag, duplicate existing tags, and embedded malicious instructions. Baseline passed 1/4 cases; revision 4 passed 3/4. The remaining case correctly replaced a placeholder title but retained incorrect date, correspondent, and type. This confirms that preservation improved more convincingly than correction.

In total, 112 case inferences were run: 96 training, eight synthetic challenge, and eight holdout. There was one baseline training run, so the repeat range describes candidate variability rather than a statistically controlled comparison.

## Why this is not ready to resume automatic OCR updates

Scan comparison found remaining omissions even when the model returned `complete:true`: an experimental check transcription omitted readable check-number and MICR text, and a receipt retained a corrupted item description. Other readable documents were left unchanged, preserving known OCR errors. The validator verifies shape and scope, not whether a transcription is factually complete.

The fact rubric catches some omissions but cannot certify a whole document. Formatting differences can cause false negatives, while short substrings can cause false positives. The strict all-text OCR policy can also force abstention when a small region is uncertain. Do not interpret the four-document holdout or its 19/23 fact score as production accuracy.

Next acceptance work should separate preservation from correction, use reviewed full-text or region references, and evaluate difficult-page rendering or a dedicated OCR pass with a completeness check. Compare any stronger-model fallback on the same training cases. Use a fresh reserved holdout for the next tuning round and run the final candidate through the actual Docker/Pi path before deployment.

The worker's journal still records previously completed jobs after Paperless restoration. A future controlled reprocess must explicitly select those documents and retain the existing journal/audit evidence; deleting the state volume would lose recovery history.
